# In-plane Springs printed flat, with separate T-pads

Each Spring is a parallel-guided flexure cut into the Wall's own plane: vertical Beams between a Top bar and a Bottom bar, so the T-pad slides straight in and out without tilting. The Beams use the Wall's 60 mm height for length, which is the only way to get a soft enough Spring in a short zone. The Wall prints flat so the Beams bend along their filament lines; printed upright, they would bend across layer bonds and crack. A T-pad cannot print flat with the Wall (half of it would sit below the bed), so it is a separate clip-on part. It prints face-down: contact face on the bed, clip jaws rising 3 mm with the slot open upward. That is short, stable and overhang-free, and the contact face gets the plate's finish. (v1 printed them standing up, which works too, but they are 60 mm tall on a 4 × 10 mm footprint.)

## Considered Options

- **Leaf spring in the T-pad (the T's crossbar bends):** printable upright, but needs about 50 mm arms per side to be soft enough.
- **One-piece Wall and T-pads printed upright:** puts Beam bending stress across layer lines.
- **Rubber strips on the T-pads:** rejected by the user; grip comes from Spring tension only.
